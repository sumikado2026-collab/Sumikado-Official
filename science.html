Ingredient Research and Reading Guide

Three selected papers introduce the overall evidence, ingredient background, and human research findings and limitations.

These studies did not test Chengyun Silkie Chicken Essence. Formulations, doses, and participants differ; their findings cannot establish the same effects for this product.

Chicken essence and cognition: an evidence overview

Authors: Teoh SL, Sudfangsai S, Lumbiganon P, Laopaiboon M, Lai NM, Chaiyakunapruk N
Publication year: 2016

Research topic: A systematic review and meta-analysis of chicken essence and cognitive function.

Summary — Some executive-function analyses were positive, but limited study quality and inconsistent findings leave insufficient convincing evidence to establish cognitive benefits.

Nutrients, 8(1):57. DOI: 10.3390/nu8010057

Read the paper

Carnosine and anserine: research background

Authors: Nishitani M, Munekiyo Y, Sugino T, Kajimoto O
Publication year: 2009

Research topic: A review of imidazole dipeptides and fatigue research.

Summary — Introduces carnosine and anserine and discusses two human studies related to fatigue. This is early background literature, not a complete account of current evidence.

Japanese Journal of Complementary and Alternative Medicine, 6(3):123–129. DOI: 10.1625/jcam.6.123

Read the paper

Chicken essence and mental fatigue: a small human trial

Authors: Yamano E, Tanaka M, Ishii A, Tsuruoka N, Abe K, Watanabe Y
Publication year: 2013

Research topic: Repeated chicken essence intake and cognitive performance after mental fatigue.

Summary — Twenty healthy men took part in a placebo-controlled crossover trial. At weeks 1 and 4, reaction times after the post-fatigue rest period were shorter than at baseline; those before and immediately after the fatigue task were unchanged. Placebo showed no comparable significant change, but this alone does not establish superiority over placebo or identify a responsible ingredient.

Medical Science Monitor, 19:540–547. DOI: 10.12659/MSM.883971
